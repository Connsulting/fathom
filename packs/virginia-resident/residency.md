# Determine Virginia residency and income coverage

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

Virginia distinguishes domiciliary residents, actual residents, part-year residents and nonresidents. Domicile is the permanent legal home; living elsewhere temporarily does not necessarily abandon it. Maintaining the relevant Virginia presence or abode for more than 183 days can create actual residency even when another state remains the domicile. Residents generally report income from all sources, rather than only Virginia customers or employers.

A genuine move into or out of the state can require a part-year return. A nonresident can still owe Virginia tax on Virginia-source income. The spouse's status must be evaluated separately when the household has mixed residency, military circumstances or a move during the year.

Recommendation: retain the move timeline, domicile evidence, work locations and income allocation records before selecting a return type. Do not infer residency from a payroll mailing address alone. W-2 employment, partnership income and remote consulting can create different source questions. Route potential double taxation to the other-state credit review rather than simply omitting income from the Virginia projection.

Sources: [Virginia Tax residency status](https://www.tax.virginia.gov/residency-status), [Virginia Tax credit for taxes paid to another state](https://www.tax.virginia.gov/credit-for-taxes-paid-to-another-state).
