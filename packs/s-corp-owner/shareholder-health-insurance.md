# Shareholder health insurance and payroll reporting

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

For an employee who owns more than 2% of an S corporation, corporation-paid health premiums generally belong in Form W-2 Box 1. When the coverage meets the stated plan or system conditions, those premiums are excluded from Social Security, Medicare and FUTA wages and from Boxes 3 and 5. The income tax treatment differs from ordinary employee health coverage. [IRS S corporation compensation and medical insurance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues)

## Review

For the self-employed health insurance deduction, the corporation must establish the coverage, pay or reimburse the premiums and report them as taxable compensation. A policy in the shareholder's name can qualify if the corporation properly reimburses and reports it. Paying personally with no corporate reimbursement does not establish the arrangement. Other deduction limits, including earned income and eligibility for subsidized employer coverage, still apply. [IRS Form 7206 instructions, self-employed health insurance](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206) [IRS S corporation compensation and medical insurance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues) [IRS Publication 334, self-employed health insurance rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334)

## Trap and interaction

Check employer coverage available through the spouse by month before assuming the deduction applies. Coordinate payroll reporting with the personal return and review spouse attribution before treating an employed spouse as an ordinary employee for benefits. HSA eligibility is a separate question from deductibility of premiums.
