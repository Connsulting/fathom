# Retirement contributions use wages, not distributions

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

S corporation shareholder distributions are not earned income for retirement plan purposes. Employee deferrals and employer contributions depend on eligible Form W-2 compensation and the plan's terms. Raising distributions does not create retirement contribution room. [IRS retirement plan contributions for S corporations](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plan-faqs-regarding-contributions-s-corporation)

## 2026 limits

The ordinary 401(k) employee deferral limit is $24,500. Total defined contribution annual additions are limited to the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of eligible compensation, with qualifying catch-up contributions outside the annual additions limit. The compensation ceiling for relevant employer contribution calculations is $360,000. Employer deduction and percentage limits still apply, so the statutory dollar cap is not an automatic contribution allowance. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560)

## Deadlines and interaction

Make deferral elections before the relevant compensation becomes available and follow payroll and plan funding requirements. Do not apply the special first-year sole proprietor deferral exception to an S corporation. Employee deferrals at an outside employer consume the same individual deferral limit; use the advanced-retirement pack for aggregation and catch-up rules. Review compensation for reasonableness independently of any contribution objective. [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560) [IRS S corporation compensation and medical insurance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues)
