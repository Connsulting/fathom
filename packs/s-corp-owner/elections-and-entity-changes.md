# S elections and changes to a single-member LLC

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

An LLC's state-law form does not establish its federal tax treatment. An eligible entity can elect S corporation treatment using Form 2553; the general deadline is no more than 2 months and 15 days after the start of the tax year the election is to take effect, or during the preceding tax year. Shareholder consents and eligibility conditions apply. Late-election relief has its own requirements and is not automatic. [IRS Form 2553 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i2553) [IRS Publication 3402, LLC classification](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402)

## Transition review

A single-member LLC normally has disregarded income tax status only when no corporate election controls. Reducing an S corporation to one shareholder does not terminate its corporate tax election. Moving from corporate treatment to disregarded treatment can involve deemed liquidation and tax consequences. A legal name change or new bank account does not accomplish the tax transition. [IRS Publication 3402, LLC classification](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402)

## Trap and interaction

Before changing treatment, reconcile assets, liabilities, shareholder basis, payroll and benefit arrangements, and retirement plan sponsorship. Compare the sole proprietor rules in self-employed-partnership with the existing corporation's obligations. Do not stop payroll or reclassify receipts on the assumption that forming a solo LLC automatically ends an S election. [IRS Publication 3402, LLC classification](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402) [IRS S corporation stock and debt basis](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-stock-and-debt-basis) [IRS retirement plan contributions for S corporations](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plan-faqs-regarding-contributions-s-corporation)
