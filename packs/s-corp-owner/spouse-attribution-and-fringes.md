# Spouse attribution and owner fringe benefits

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

A more-than-2% S corporation shareholder is treated like a partner for several employee fringe benefit exclusions. Direct and indirect ownership count. Family attribution can make an employed spouse an owner for these rules even when the spouse holds no shares directly. The IRS explains spouse, child, grandchild and parent attribution in Chief Counsel Advice 201912001; that advice explains the rule but is not precedential authority. [IRS Publication 15-B, 2026 fringe benefits](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b) [IRS Chief Counsel Advice 201912001, family attribution explanation](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/201912001.pdf)

## Review

Check each benefit's own exclusion. A more-than-2% shareholder cannot receive the ordinary employee exclusion through a cafeteria plan. Health insurance, group-term life insurance, dependent care and employer HSA contributions need their respective owner treatment. Do not infer that every benefit is barred: some exclusions have different eligibility rules. [IRS Publication 15-B, 2026 fringe benefits](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b)

## Trap and interaction

Putting the spouse on payroll does not by itself restore employee exclusions. Record ownership and attribution before choosing an arrangement, and reconcile any taxable benefits with payroll. Coordinate the health deduction with the shareholder-health-insurance entry and the spouse's outside employer coverage. [IRS Chief Counsel Advice 201912001, family attribution explanation](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/201912001.pdf) [IRS S corporation compensation and medical insurance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues)
