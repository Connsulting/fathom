# Use the correct HSA year and coverage limits

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

For 2026, the HSA contribution ceiling is $4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family coverage, before an eligible age-55 catch-up. These totals include employer funding and employee funding; moving money between payroll and a personal deposit does not create extra room. Apply the eligibility-month rules, the shared family limit for eligible spouses, and any catch-up separately.

An ordinary 2026 HDHP has a minimum deductible of $1,700 self-only or $3,400 family and an out-of-pocket ceiling of $8,500 or $17,000. The special bronze and catastrophic rules require separate attention. The last-month rule can permit a larger contribution but brings a following-year testing period, with tax consequences if eligibility fails.

Published 2027 limits are different: HSA contributions $4,500 self-only and $9,000 family; ordinary HDHP deductibles $1,750 and $3,500; out-of-pocket ceilings $8,700 and $17,400. Use the contribution's designated tax year, rather than the date a payroll system happens to display a new election.

Sources: [IRS Publication 15-B (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b), [IRS Publication 969: enduring HSA and FSA rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), [IRS Notice 2026-05: HSA eligibility changes](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-02_IRB), [IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-24: published 2027 HSA limits](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-25_irb).
