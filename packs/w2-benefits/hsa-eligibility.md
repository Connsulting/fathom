# Establish HSA eligibility before contributing

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

HSA eligibility is determined by coverage and personal status, generally month by month. An eligible person needs qualifying high deductible health coverage, no disqualifying additional coverage, no Medicare enrollment and no eligibility to be claimed as another taxpayer's dependent. A general-purpose health FSA or HRA that covers the person generally prevents HSA contributions. Limited-purpose or qualifying post-deductible arrangements can be compatible.

Check the spouse's coverage as well as the employee's election. Owning an HSA, or seeing an HSA payroll option, does not prove eligibility for another contribution. Losing contribution eligibility does not erase an existing HSA balance or automatically prevent qualified withdrawals.

For 2026, Notice 2026-05 adds important exceptions: qualifying individual-market bronze and catastrophic plans can count as HDHPs under its conditions, and certain direct primary care arrangements need not disqualify otherwise eligible individuals. Do not apply older Publication 969 coverage language without these updates. Recommendation: retain the actual coverage documents and eligibility months before funding or correcting payroll contributions.

Sources: [IRS Publication 969: enduring HSA and FSA rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), [IRS Publication 15-B (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b), [IRS Notice 2026-05: HSA eligibility changes](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-02_IRB).
