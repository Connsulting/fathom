# Treatment by account type

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-07

Pre-tax treatment covers a traditional IRA, a SEP IRA, a pre-tax 401(k), a 403(b), a 457(b) and a solo 401(k). Contributions are deductible or pre-tax. Growth is tax-deferred. Withdrawals are ordinary income. Required minimum distributions apply.

A Roth IRA takes after-tax contributions. Growth is tax-free. Contribution basis can come out any time tax and penalty free. Earnings are tax-free after age 59 and a half and five years. The original owner has no required minimum distribution.

A Roth 401(k) takes after-tax contributions. Growth is tax-free. A qualified withdrawal needs age 59 and a half and five years. The original owner has had no required minimum distribution since 2024.

A retirement or IRA account whose plan type is not recorded does not have a treatment yet. That gap is needs plan type. Checking and cash are cash. A taxable or brokerage account is taxable. An HSA is an HSA. A 529 is a 529. A credit card is a liability. A business account is business. A loan is a loan. None of those last three is a retirement tax bucket.

Sources: [IRS Publication 590-A: contributions to individual retirement arrangements](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a). [IRS Publication 575: pension and annuity income](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p575).
