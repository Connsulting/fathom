# Penalty-free access

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-07

The usual penalty-free age for a retirement distribution is 59 and a half. Employer plans have an extra exception called the rule of 55: separation from that employer in or after the year of turning 55. The employer plan types are 401(k), Roth 401(k), solo 401(k), 403(b) and 457(b). The rule of 55 does not apply to IRAs.

A governmental 457(b) has a separate rule. After separation from service, its distributions carry no 10 percent early-withdrawal additional tax at any age. Amounts rolled in from other plan types do not get that exception. The plan type 457b cannot tell a governmental 457(b) from a non-governmental one, so the governmental rule needs that fact. Without it, do not treat the account as penalty-free at any age.

Substantially equal periodic payments are a separate exception. This entry states the ages. It does not compute a household date.
