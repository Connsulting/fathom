# Compare the care credit with dependent care assistance

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

For 2026, the nonrefundable child and dependent care credit uses up to $3,000 of qualifying expenses for one qualifying person or $6,000 for two or more. Its applicable rate now ranges from 20% to 50%, depending on adjusted gross income. Do not carry forward the older maximum 35% rate. A nonrefundable credit cannot exceed the applicable tax liability.

Employer dependent care assistance can instead exclude up to $7,500 for 2026, or $3,750 for married filing separately, subject to earned-income and other restrictions. Combine benefits across both employers. The employer's plan may offer less than the statutory maximum, and an election is not proof that the full amount will be tax-exempt.

Excluded assistance reduces the expenses available for the credit; the same care cannot support both benefits. A fully used exclusion can exhaust the credit's smaller expense ceiling. Recommendation: compare the actual household's coordinated tax result before electing, using qualifying expenses, both spouses' earned income, plan deadlines and filing status. Preserve Form W-2 box 10 and reimbursement records for Form 2441.

Sources: [IRS child and dependent care credit: 2026 changes](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-and-dependent-care-credit-information), [IRS Publication 15-B (2026): dependent care assistance](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b), [IRS Publication 503: enduring qualifying-care rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p503).
