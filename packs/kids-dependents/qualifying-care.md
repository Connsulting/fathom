# Identify care expenses that allow work

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

Child and dependent care benefits generally concern expenses that allow the taxpayer and spouse, if filing jointly, to work or actively look for work. A dependent child under age 13 when the care is provided can be a qualifying person. Special rules can cover a spouse or another qualifying person who cannot care for themselves. These tests differ from the child tax credit's age test.

Separate qualifying care from education and personal spending. Preschool and qualifying day care can meet the care rules; school tuition and overnight camps have different treatment. Keep service dates, the child's age during care, invoices and the provider's identifying information. The person providing care also matters: payments to a spouse, certain dependents or certain close family members are restricted.

Recommendation: review leave periods, changes in work and the spouse's earned income before estimating the benefit. Student and disability exceptions may apply but must be established. A receipt alone proves payment, not the work-related purpose or the provider's eligibility under the tax rules.

Sources: [IRS Publication 503: enduring qualifying-care rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p503), [IRS child and dependent care credit: 2026 changes](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-and-dependent-care-credit-information).
