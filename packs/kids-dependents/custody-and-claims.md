# Separate custody rules from credit allocation

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

When parents live apart, determine which parent is the custodial parent under the federal nights-of-residence rules. Paying most expenses or being named in a private agreement does not automatically establish the right to every child-related tax benefit. A child's residence, support and possible competing claims need a documented review.

A qualifying release using Form 8332 can allow the noncustodial parent to claim specified dependent-related benefits, including the child tax credit where its requirements are met. It does not transfer every benefit: head-of-household status, earned income credit and dependent care benefits can remain tied to the custodial parent's separate eligibility rules. A release therefore cannot simply be copied into every child-related worksheet.

Recommendation: retain the custody calendar, applicable release and provider records, and resolve competing claims before filing. Even in an intact household, a child temporarily living with relatives can create a residence or tie-breaker question. Recheck dependent status and each benefit after a separation, move or placement change instead of preserving last year's claimant by default.

Sources: [IRS Publication 501: enduring dependent and custody rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501), [IRS Publication 503: enduring qualifying-care rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p503).
