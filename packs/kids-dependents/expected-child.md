# Treat an expected child as a future life event

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

An expected child is a planning event, not an existing dependent before birth. If the child is expected in a later tax year, do not add a 2026 child credit merely because pregnancy or preparatory expenses occur during 2026. Maintain the expected event separately from the facts used to determine the current-year return.

When a child is born during a tax year, the special residency rule can treat the child as living with the taxpayer for more than half that year if the home was the child's home for more than half the time the child was alive, subject to the other dependency tests. A late-year birth therefore requires reviewing the full-year rules rather than prorating a child credit by months.

Recommendation: revisit employer coverage, dependent care elections, household cash needs and withholding after the event is confirmed. Obtain the child's required identification and preserve birth and residence records. Check each benefit's actual enrollment or election procedure; the tax residency exception does not itself enroll a child in insurance or change payroll elections.

Sources: [IRS Publication 501: enduring dependent and custody rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501), [IRS Publication 505 (2026): withholding changes](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505).
