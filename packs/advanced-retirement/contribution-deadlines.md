# Retirement elections, funding and reporting deadlines

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## IRA deadline

Ordinary 2026 IRA contributions can generally be made through April 15, 2027. A personal filing extension generally does not extend the regular IRA contribution deadline. Designate the intended tax year when contributing during the following calendar year. A conversion follows its distribution and conversion reporting rules rather than the prior-year contribution designation. [IRS Forms 1099-R and 5498 instructions for 2026](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099r) [IRS Publication 590-A, IRA contributions and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a)

## Employer plan deadlines

A SEP can generally be adopted and funded by the employer's tax return due date including extensions. For an established owner plan, deferral elections generally must be in place by year-end and before the compensation becomes available; funding and employer deduction deadlines are separate. Payroll deferrals must also satisfy applicable deposit rules. A return extension does not authorize retroactive payroll elections. [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560)

## Narrow first-year exception

A sole proprietor with no employees can adopt a new 401(k) after year-end under the special first-year rule. The only-participant deferrals must be paid by the unextended individual return deadline. Do not apply that exception to an S corporation, a partnership or an established plan. Confirm plan terms and custodian processing cutoffs before relying on the statutory deadline. [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560)

## Reporting

One-participant plans generally must file Form 5500-EZ when combined plan assets exceed $250,000 at year-end; a final plan year generally requires filing even below that threshold. Funding a plan and filing its annual report are different obligations. [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560) [IRS one-participant 401(k) plans](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/one-participant-401k-plans)
