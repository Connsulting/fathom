# IRA contribution, deduction and Roth eligibility

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Contribution limit

For 2026, combined regular traditional and Roth IRA contributions generally cannot exceed $7,500, or $8,600 at age 50 or older, and may be limited by eligible compensation. Each spouse has an individual IRA limit; a joint return with sufficient joint compensation may support a spousal IRA even when one spouse has little compensation. Eligibility to contribute and eligibility to deduct are separate questions. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS Publication 590-A, IRA contributions and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a)

## 2026 income ranges

Direct Roth contribution eligibility phases out at modified AGI of $153,000 to $168,000 for single and head-of-household filers, and $242,000 to $252,000 for married filing jointly. Married filing separately while living with the spouse generally uses the $0 to $10,000 range. Separate filers who lived apart all year have different treatment. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS Publication 590-A, IRA contributions and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a)

For a contributor covered by a workplace plan, traditional IRA deduction phase-outs are $81,000 to $91,000 for single or head of household and $129,000 to $149,000 for a joint return. A joint filer not covered by a plan but married to a covered spouse generally uses $242,000 to $252,000. Separate returns have special rules. Use modified AGI under the applicable IRA calculation, not an unadjusted income fact. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS Publication 590-A, IRA contributions and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a)

## Interaction

An employer retirement plan does not automatically prohibit an IRA contribution. It can limit the deduction. A nondeductible contribution requires basis records and may interact with backdoor Roth planning. [IRS Publication 590-A, IRA contributions and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a) [IRS Form 8606 instructions, IRA basis and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8606)
