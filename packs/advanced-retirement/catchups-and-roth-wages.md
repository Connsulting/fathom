# Catch-up contributions and the 2026 Roth wage rule

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## 2026 limits

For most applicable employer plans, the ordinary age-50-or-older catch-up is $8,000. Someone attaining age 60, 61, 62 or 63 during 2026 may use $11,250 instead, where permitted. These amounts are alternatives, not cumulative additions. IRA catch-ups follow a separate $1,100 limit and SIMPLE plans have different catch-up rules. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS retirement topics, catch-up contributions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-catch-up-contributions)

## Roth requirement

Beginning in 2026, the Roth catch-up rule applies when the participant's prior-year wages with the plan sponsor exceeded $150,000 for the relevant test. For 2026, examine applicable 2025 wages. This is not a household AGI threshold and does not turn all regular elective deferrals into Roth contributions. Confirm how the plan administers the requirement and that the required Roth feature is available. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS retirement topics, catch-up contributions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-catch-up-contributions)

## Owner and spouse interaction

A partner or other self-employed person with no applicable FICA wages is different from an S corporation shareholder receiving wages. IRS guidance distinguishes self-employment income from wages for this test. Determine each spouse's own age, wages and plan eligibility; do not substitute combined household income. Catch-up limits also do not create extra voluntary after-tax capacity for a mega backdoor transaction.
