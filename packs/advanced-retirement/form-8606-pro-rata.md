# Form 8606 and the IRA pro-rata rule

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

Form 8606 tracks nondeductible traditional IRA basis and computes the taxable and nontaxable portions of distributions and conversions. Traditional IRAs generally include traditional SEP IRAs and traditional SIMPLE IRAs. The calculation looks across the owner's applicable IRAs rather than allowing basis to be assigned only to the account selected for conversion. A spouse's separately owned IRAs are a separate computation. [IRS Form 8606 instructions, IRA basis and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8606)

## Year-end review

Review the applicable December 31 value in the conversion year, along with basis, relevant distributions, conversions and outstanding rollovers. For a 2026 conversion, this means reviewing the applicable year-end 2026 balances, even if the conversion account itself is empty. A traditional SEP contribution or pretax rollover received before year-end can change the result. Use the year's final form and instructions when preparing the return; the currently published 2025 instructions establish the enduring aggregation and year-end method. [IRS Form 8606 instructions, IRA basis and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8606)

## Trap and interaction

Nondeductible basis is not the same as account market value. Do not describe a conversion as wholly tax free without the full calculation. An employer plan's acceptance of a pretax IRA rollover is plan-specific, and inherited IRAs or other special arrangements require their own treatment. Coordinate retirement plan selection and rollover timing before assessing backdoor Roth taxability. [IRS Publication 590-A, IRA contributions and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a) [IRS Form 8606 instructions, IRA basis and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8606)
