# Solo 401(k) versus SEP

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

A Solo 401(k) is an ordinary 401(k) covering a business owner with no employees, or the owner and spouse. It can permit both employee deferrals and employer contributions. A standard SEP primarily provides employer contributions; it does not provide the ordinary 401(k) employee deferral bucket or its age-based catch-up. An existing SARSEP is a separate arrangement. [IRS one-participant 401(k) plans](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/one-participant-401k-plans) [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560)

## 2026 limits and compensation

The ordinary 401(k) employee deferral cap is $24,500, shared with applicable outside plans. The annual additions cap is $72,000 before eligible catch-up contributions. SEP employer contributions generally cannot exceed 25% of eligible employee compensation or $72,000. Self-employed owners need the adjusted earned-income computation; the equivalent maximum employer deduction rate is generally 20% of net earnings after the deductible self-employment tax adjustment and before the contribution itself. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560)

## Trap and interaction

A traditional SEP IRA adds to the IRA balances relevant to a backdoor Roth conversion. A Solo 401(k) has plan administration, eligibility and reporting obligations, and hiring an eligible employee changes its treatment. Neither choice is universally better. Compare compensation, employee coverage, deadlines, provider plan terms and IRA aggregation before making a selection.
