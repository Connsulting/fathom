# Mega backdoor Roth and after-tax plan features

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

A mega backdoor Roth generally involves voluntary employee after-tax non-Roth contributions to an employer plan, followed by a permitted Roth conversion or rollover. Those contributions are different from designated Roth salary deferrals. Merely offering a Roth 401(k) option does not establish that the plan supports this strategy. [IRS retirement topics, contribution types](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-contributions) [IRS designated Roth account FAQs](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-faqs-on-designated-roth-accounts)

## Plan review

Confirm that the written plan permits voluntary after-tax contributions and provides an applicable in-plan Roth rollover or an eligible distribution that can be rolled to a Roth IRA. Ask which sources may be converted, how often and whether in-service distributions are available. Nondiscrimination testing and plan-specific caps can restrict after-tax contributions. [IRS designated Roth account FAQs](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-faqs-on-designated-roth-accounts) [IRS 401(k) nondiscrimination testing guidance](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/401k-plan-fix-it-guide-the-plan-failed-the-401k-adp-and-acp-nondiscrimination-tests)

## 2026 capacity and tax treatment

Employee deferrals, employer contributions and voluntary after-tax contributions generally share the $72,000 annual additions cap, also subject to compensation limits; qualifying catch-up contributions are treated separately. Do not infer remaining capacity without the full contribution record. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560)

After-tax basis and pretax earnings have different tax treatment. IRS guidance permits appropriate allocation of an eligible distribution among destinations, but does not allow a participant to disregard the pretax share of a partial distribution. Earnings converted to Roth are generally taxable. Pretax amounts placed in a traditional IRA can later affect a regular backdoor Roth pro-rata calculation. [IRS rollovers of after-tax retirement plan contributions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/rollovers-of-after-tax-contributions-in-retirement-plans) [IRS Form 8606 instructions, IRA basis and conversions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8606)
