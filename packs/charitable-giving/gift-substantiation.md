# Obtain the right receipts before filing

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

Keep a bank record or appropriate written communication for monetary gifts, even when the amount is small. For a contribution of $250 or more, obtain the required contemporaneous written acknowledgment from the charity. It must identify the contribution and state whether the charity supplied goods or services, with the required description and value where relevant.

Contemporaneous generally means obtained by the earlier of filing the return or its due date, including extensions. A canceled check alone does not replace the acknowledgment required for a larger gift. Separate contributions are generally tested separately for the $250 threshold, while other reporting thresholds can require aggregation.

If the household receives dinner, tickets, merchandise or another benefit, generally only the excess above the benefit's fair market value can qualify. Personal gifts, political contributions and the value of volunteered time do not become deductible merely because they support a worthy purpose. Recommendation: collect missing acknowledgments before filing and reconcile receipts to cash, property and donor-advised-fund records without counting grants from an already funded account again.

Sources: [IRS Publication 526: enduring charitable contribution rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526), [IRS Publication 6196: 2026 nonitemizer deduction](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p6196.pdf).
