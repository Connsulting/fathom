# Review gifts of appreciated stock

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

Publicly traded stock held for more than one year can be capital-gain property. A qualifying contribution to an appropriate public charity generally starts with fair market value rather than the original purchase cost, subject to deduction adjustments and limits. Stock held for a shorter period can produce a different result because its appreciation would be short-term gain.

Capital-gain property donated at fair market value to the relevant public charities is generally subject to a 30% adjusted-gross-income limit. Other recipients, property types and elections change the calculation. The 2026 itemized charitable floor and overall deduction limitation must also be applied; the percentage ceiling alone does not determine current savings.

Recommendation: verify the actual tax lots, holding period, basis and recipient's transfer instructions before initiating a gift. Compare transferring shares with selling first and giving cash as distinct tax transactions. Retain evidence of the completed transfer and valuation date. Publication 561 generally values actively traded shares using the average of the day's highest and lowest quoted selling prices, not simply the closing price.

Sources: [IRS Publication 526: enduring charitable contribution rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526), [IRS Publication 561: donated property valuation](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561), [IRS Publication 505 (2026): charitable deduction changes](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505).
