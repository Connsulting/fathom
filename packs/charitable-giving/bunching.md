# Evaluate bunching across tax years

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

Bunching means concentrating charitable contributions in selected years while maintaining a separate schedule for charitable support. It can help a household exceed the standard-deduction comparison and, beginning in 2026, the itemized charitable floor. It does not turn a gift into a dollar-for-dollar credit or guarantee that the whole contribution will be deductible immediately.

For 2026, the basic federal standard deduction is $32,200 for married filing jointly and qualifying surviving spouses, $24,150 for heads of household, and $16,100 for single or married filing separately. Additional amounts and special rules can apply for age, blindness or dependent status. These are the tax-year amounts to use when comparing bunching with the standard deduction. [IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, section 4.14](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf)

Compare the household's projected itemized deductions, adjusted gross income, available contribution limits and existing carryforwards for each affected year. Include the eligible nonitemizer cash deduction in years without itemizing. The 0.5% floor and overall itemized-deduction limitation can change the comparison, so a projection based solely on older Publication 526 examples is incomplete for 2026.

Recommendation: compare direct gifts with a donor-advised fund only after confirming recipient eligibility, fees, legal control and the household's intended giving schedule. Preserve sufficient cash for taxes and other obligations. For a Virginia household, review the linked federal and state deduction choices separately. Complete transfers within the intended tax year and retain evidence rather than treating an unfulfilled pledge as a contribution.

Sources: [IRS Publication 505 (2026): charitable deduction changes](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505), [IRS Publication 526: enduring charitable contribution rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526), [IRS Publication 6196: 2026 nonitemizer deduction](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p6196.pdf).
