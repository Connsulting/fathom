# Apply the new 2026 charitable deduction framework

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

Beginning in 2026, taxpayers who do not itemize may deduct eligible cash contributions up to $1,000, or $2,000 on a married filing jointly return, subject to the recipient and other restrictions. Contributions to donor-advised funds generally do not qualify for this nonitemizer deduction. A qualifying charitable purpose alone is not enough; confirm the receiving organization's eligibility and keep payment records.

For taxpayers who itemize, 2026 introduces a floor of 0.5% of adjusted gross income. Charitable contributions below that floor do not create a current-year itemized charitable deduction. Percentage-of-income ceilings and the new overall itemized-deduction limitation can impose further restrictions. Do not estimate savings by applying a marginal tax rate to the entire gift.

For 2026, qualifying cash gifts to public charities generally face a ceiling of 60% of AGI, with contribution-base adjustments and coordination rules for other gifts. Amounts over the applicable ceiling can generally carry forward for up to five succeeding tax years, subject to the applicable rules. The 60% cash ceiling does not apply to appreciated stock or every recipient category. Apply the 0.5% itemized floor and the separate overall itemized-deduction limit as well. [Internal Revenue Code 170(b)(1)(G) and (b)(1)(I)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s170/) [IRS Publication 526: contribution limits and carryovers](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526)

Recommendation: decide the giving budget first, then compare deduction methods using the household's full projected return. Keep cash gifts, property gifts, donor-advised contributions and qualified charitable distributions in separate records because their treatment differs. Revisit the projection after business income, capital gains or Roth conversions change adjusted gross income.

Sources: [IRS Publication 505 (2026): charitable deduction changes](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505), [IRS Publication 6196: 2026 nonitemizer deduction](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p6196.pdf).
