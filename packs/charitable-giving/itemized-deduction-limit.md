# Apply the precise 2026 overall itemized-deduction limit

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

Beginning in 2026, section 68 reduces otherwise allowable itemized deductions by 2/37 of the lesser of two amounts: those itemized deductions, or the positive excess over the start of the 37% bracket of taxable income computed without section 68 and then increased by those itemized deductions. That income measure adds the deductions back; it is not simply final taxable income after itemization.

The 2026 bracket threshold is $768,700 for married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse, $640,600 for single or head of household, and $384,350 for married filing separately. Below the applicable income measure threshold there is no section 68 reduction. The statutory fraction is 2/37; Publication 505 uses 5.4% as an estimated-tax worksheet approximation.

Apply section 68 after other limits on itemized deductions, including the SALT cap and charitable contribution limits and floor. It does not limit the qualified business income deduction. Recommendation: compare charitable bunching using the coordinated return result, rather than assuming every deductible dollar saves the top 37% rate. Keep the federal limit separate from Virginia's own itemized-deduction treatment.

Sources: [Internal Revenue Code 68: exact reduction and ordering](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s68/), [IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32: 2026 brackets](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf), [IRS Publication 505 (2026): overall limitation and Worksheet 2-6](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505).
