# Use qualified charitable distributions only when eligible

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

A qualified charitable distribution generally must move directly from an eligible IRA's trustee to an eligible charity. The IRA owner or eligible beneficiary must be at least age 70 1/2 when the distribution occurs. Ongoing SEP and SIMPLE IRAs are excluded under the applicable rules. A distribution to the household followed by a personal gift is not automatically a QCD.

The 2026 annual QCD exclusion limit is $111,000 per eligible individual, aggregated across their IRAs. Eligible spouses apply their own limits to their own distributions. Donor-advised funds and specified supporting organizations are ineligible recipients. Retain the charity's acknowledgment and the custodian's distribution records.

An excluded QCD cannot also be claimed as an itemized charitable deduction. A qualifying distribution can count toward an applicable required minimum distribution, but the timing and account rules still matter. Deductible IRA contributions made at or after age 70 1/2 can reduce the excludable QCD amount under the anti-abuse calculation. Recommendation: coordinate the transfer with retirement distributions before year-end and confirm the return reports the exclusion correctly.

Sources: [IRS Publication 590-B: enduring QCD rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b), [IRS Notice 2025-67: 2026 QCD limit](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf), [IRS Notice 2007-7: QCD recipient and distribution rules](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-05_IRB).
