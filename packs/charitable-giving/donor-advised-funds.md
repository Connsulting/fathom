# Understand donor-advised fund control and deduction timing

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

A donor-advised fund is maintained by a sponsoring charitable organization. After a contribution, the sponsoring organization has legal control of the assets while the donor can retain advisory privileges over distributions or investments. Treat funding as a charitable commitment rather than a household reserve that can later be reclaimed for personal expenses.

A deductible contribution requires the applicable contemporaneous acknowledgment, including the sponsor's exclusive legal control. The contribution's tax treatment depends on the property, sponsor and deduction limits. A later grant recommended from the fund does not create another household charitable contribution deduction for the same assets. Keep the original funding record separate from grant activity.

For 2026, donor-advised fund contributions generally do not qualify for the nonitemizer cash deduction. They are also ineligible recipients for a qualified charitable distribution from an IRA. Recommendation: verify the sponsor, fee structure, accepted assets and grant restrictions before comparing a fund with direct giving. Bunching may be useful, but the 2026 itemized floor and other limits still require a full return calculation.

Sources: [IRS donor-advised funds guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/donor-advised-funds), [IRS Publication 526: enduring charitable contribution rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526), [IRS Publication 6196: 2026 nonitemizer deduction](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p6196.pdf), [IRS Notice 2007-7: QCD recipient and distribution rules](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-05_IRB).
