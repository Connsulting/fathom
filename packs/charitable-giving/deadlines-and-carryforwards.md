# Track completion dates and charitable carryforwards

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

A charitable deduction generally belongs to the year the contribution is actually made. An unpaid pledge is insufficient. The usual rule turns on unconditional delivery, with specific rules for checks, credit cards and securities. Recommendation: initiate property and custodian transfers early enough to confirm the actual completion date; an instruction sent before year-end is not always a completed gift.

Ordinary contributions exceeding applicable adjusted-gross-income percentage ceilings can generally carry forward for up to five succeeding years, with special rules for certain categories. Track each carryforward's origin year, property category, limitation and remaining life. Current-year contributions and carryovers interact under ordering rules; do not combine everything into one unrestricted balance.

For 2026, the new 0.5% itemized floor introduces additional treatment that should not be confused with the older percentage-limit carryover rules. Do not assume every amount below the floor automatically carries forward. Use current return instructions for that calculation. Reconcile planned gifts, completed transfers, acknowledgments and prior carryforwards before adjusting estimated tax or wage withholding for an expected deduction.

Sources: [IRS Publication 526: enduring charitable contribution rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526), [IRS Publication 505 (2026): charitable deduction changes](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505).
