# Value noncash gifts and complete the required reporting

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

A noncash gift requires evidence of the donated property, its condition, acquisition and basis, contribution date and fair market value. A purchase price, sentimental value or an unsigned estimate from the recipient does not automatically establish the deduction. Different property categories have different restrictions, so separate publicly traded securities, household goods, vehicles and unusual assets.

When total noncash deductions exceed $500, Form 8283 is generally required. A deduction over $5,000 for an item or group of similar items generally requires a qualified appraisal and Section B, subject to exceptions including publicly traded securities. Similar items may need aggregation even when contributed to different organizations. Digital assets do not receive the publicly traded securities exception merely because exchange prices are available.

Recommendation: arrange any appraisal and recipient acknowledgment before the filing deadline rather than discovering the requirements during return preparation. Retain the valuation method and supporting evidence with the donation record. Apply the 2026 floor and applicable percentage limits after establishing the allowable value; a valid appraisal does not make an otherwise restricted amount currently deductible.

Sources: [IRS Publication 526: enduring charitable contribution rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526), [IRS Publication 561: donated property valuation](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561), [IRS Publication 505 (2026): charitable deduction changes](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505).
