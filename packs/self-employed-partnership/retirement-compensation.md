# Business retirement plans and adjusted earned income

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

A sole proprietor's or partner's retirement contribution capacity comes from eligible net earnings, not gross receipts or owner draws. The self-employed calculation adjusts for the deductible portion of self-employment tax and for contributions on the owner's behalf. Use the IRS worksheet or a deterministic calculator that implements it. For partners, the partnership is the employer maintaining the plan. [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560) [IRS calculation of plan compensation for partnerships](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/calculation-of-plan-compensation-for-partnerships)

## 2026 limits

The ordinary 401(k) employee deferral limit is $24,500 and the defined contribution annual additions cap is $72,000 before eligible catch-up contributions. SEP employer contributions also face a compensation percentage limit and the applicable annual cap. These dollar ceilings do not establish that a particular business has enough earned income to contribute them. [IRS Notice 2025-67, 2026 retirement limits](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf) [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560)

## Deadlines and interaction

A SEP can generally be established and funded by the employer's return deadline including extensions. An established Solo 401(k) has separate election and funding requirements; a narrow first-year sole proprietor exception does not apply to every business. Before choosing a SEP, review its traditional IRA balance effect on backdoor Roth conversions and the employee coverage rules in advanced-retirement. [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560)
