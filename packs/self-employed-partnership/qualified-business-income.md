# QBI for sole proprietors and partners

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

An eligible owner may deduct up to 20% of qualified business income, subject to taxable income and other limits. Partner guaranteed payments for services are excluded from QBI. Business-related deductions such as deductible self-employment tax, self-employed health insurance and qualified retirement contributions can reduce QBI. Use the business and household facts, not simply gross receipts or cash distributions. [IRS qualified business income deduction](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/qualified-business-income-deduction) [IRS Form 8995 instructions, enduring QBI definitions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995)

## 2026 thresholds

The taxable income threshold before QBI is $403,500 for joint returns, $201,775 for married filing separately and $201,750 for other returns. The respective phase-in ranges end at $553,500, $276,775 and $276,750. Specified service business and wage or property limits can matter within and above these ranges. AGI alone does not establish where the household falls. [IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, 2026 inflation adjustments](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf) [IRS qualified business income deduction](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/qualified-business-income-deduction)

## Trap and interaction

For 2026 a $400 minimum deduction may apply with at least $1,000 of active qualified business income, subject to eligibility. Retirement and health deductions can lower income tax while changing QBI, so compare the combined result in deterministic calculations. A change to S corporation treatment also changes compensation and payroll obligations; QBI is one part of that review. [IRS Publication 505, 2026 estimated tax](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505) [IRS Form 8995 instructions, enduring QBI definitions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995)
