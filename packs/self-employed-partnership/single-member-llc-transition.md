# Transition from a partnership to a solo LLC

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

When an LLC taxed as a partnership drops to one member, it generally becomes a disregarded entity unless an existing corporate election controls. An individually owned single-member LLC commonly reports an operating trade or business on Schedule C. The membership change can terminate the partnership for federal tax purposes even while the state-law LLC continues. [IRS Publication 3402, LLC classification](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402)

## Transition review

Identify the transaction that removes the other owner. A purchase, redemption or distribution can have different gain, basis and liability consequences. Reconcile final partnership activity, final K-1s, asset basis, owner liabilities and the continuing business's reporting. File the appropriate final business return and confirm which payroll and information return obligations continue. [IRS Publication 3402, LLC classification](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402) [IRS Publication 541, partnerships](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541) [IRS closing a business](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/closing-a-business)

## Trap and interaction

This change is different from an S corporation converting to disregarded status. An existing corporate election does not disappear when membership becomes one. Recheck health insurance reporting, estimated tax, retirement plan employer identity and employee coverage after the transition. Do not carry a partnership's contribution or benefit treatment into a sole proprietorship without reviewing the new facts. [IRS Publication 3402, LLC classification](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402) [IRS Publication 560, small business retirement plans](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p560) [IRS Publication 334, self-employed health insurance rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334)
