# Sole proprietor income and self-employment tax

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

A sole proprietor generally reports business income and expenses on Schedule C and computes applicable self-employment tax on Schedule SE. An individually owned single-member LLC with disregarded status generally follows the same income tax treatment. Owner draws do not replace the business's net profit and do not create wage expense for the owner. [IRS self-employed individuals tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) [IRS Publication 3402, LLC classification](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402)

## Review

Reconcile gross receipts, ordinary business expenses and net profit before estimating household tax. Self-employment tax is separate from income tax. A person generally files Schedule SE when net self-employment earnings are $400 or more, although other return filing requirements can apply below that amount. Keep personal costs separate from deductible business costs. [IRS self-employed individuals tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center)

## Trap and interaction

Receiving a Form 1099 is not the sole test for whether receipts are business income, and leaving money in the business does not defer tax on its profit. Changes to profit affect estimated tax, retirement compensation, health insurance deductions and QBI. W-2 wages elsewhere can affect the Social Security portion of the self-employment calculation; calculate from the combined facts rather than treating every business as an independent annual cap. [IRS self-employed individuals tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) [IRS Publication 505, 2026 estimated tax](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505)
