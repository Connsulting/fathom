# Self-employed and partner health insurance

Tax year: 2026

Reviewed: 2026-11-05

## Rule

The self-employed health insurance deduction has eligibility and earned-income limits; paying a premium personally does not by itself establish deductibility. The treatment depends on whether the taxpayer is a sole proprietor, partner or more-than-2% S corporation shareholder. It is generally an adjustment to income rather than a Schedule C business expense for the owner's own coverage. [IRS Form 7206 instructions, self-employed health insurance](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206) [IRS Publication 334, self-employed health insurance rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334)

## Partnership review

When a partnership pays health premiums for services as a partner, the premiums are generally guaranteed payments included in the partner's income. The partnership may deduct them as a business expense. Treating the payment simply as a reduction in distributions changes the partnership deduction. The partner then separately applies the self-employed health deduction rules. If a policy is in the partner's name and the partner pays personally, the partnership must reimburse and report the premiums as guaranteed payments to establish the business arrangement. [IRS Form 7206 instructions, self-employed health insurance](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206) [IRS Publication 541, partnerships](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541)

## Trap and interaction

No deduction is allowed for a month, or part of a month, when the taxpayer is eligible for a subsidized employer health plan through the taxpayer, spouse or other qualifying family member. Eligibility matters even if the taxpayer declined that coverage. Reconcile premium records, K-1 treatment and the deduction; review HSA eligibility separately. A later S election requires different payroll reporting described in s-corp-owner. [IRS Publication 541, partnerships](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541) [IRS Publication 334, self-employed health insurance rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334)
